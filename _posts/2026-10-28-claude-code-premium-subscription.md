The premium Claude Code subscription changed my life. I've rediscovered vibe coding for myself once again.

The first wave was around February, and back then it seemed like nothing could stop me. But it all slowly fizzled out: I kept running into limits, trying to save money, playing around with Chinese models.

And then a month ago I forced myself to switch to the $100 plan.

I used to think: anything is possible, but maybe not right now.

And now I think: **anything is possible, and I want it now!**

Every idea finds its energy. This ease of experimenting with any random thing and solving any task frees up your hands. I recommend it to everyone!

It's an incredible feeling to have a small team of very smart people ready to work for food. Now I hand off any crap to the agent.

Go into the browser and update the API keys there? Go for it, I don't touch that anymore.

Set something up in some dashboard? Not me. Come on, dudes, get to work.

Write ten articles? Figure it out yourselves, I'll look at the result and give feedback.

Recently I got into making videos, and it's just out of this world. Just as an experiment, for kicks, I think: okay, let's do a series of ten short videos, three or four minutes each, about my product. And the next day I've got ten cool, interesting videos. I've already started posting them, [you can check them out](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLANi9zLWuEYs&si=DYF3YzM7MX2I2A_0). I really like them.

But wait, this is actually **a training course**! I can put it up on some e-learning platform. The content quality is good. It's really useful for beginners, and what I'm doing is shown clearly. Right then I tell the agent: find the top platforms where we can upload the course. And right away I give the assignment: sign up, prepare drafts.

The next day I think: wait, these could also be articles for our site, for SEO. Not everybody watches videos, not everybody needs a course, but this way we'll have a huge section for beginners on our site. And the next day I've got **10 articles with illustrations** and all the details ready.

Then I look at the articles: it doesn't use real microcontrollers, only a third-party emulator. And I think: why are we advertising some third-party service? I can build that myself. Let Lopaka have it. And the next day Lopaka has **a source code emulator** for pretty much every platform.

Then my USB mic glitched out. Dude, investigate, figure it out, we'll fix it. Half an hour later I've got options for how to fix it.

So I just daydream: damn, what would be cool to build? And I don't stop to think about whether it's hard or whether I'll have enough tokens. I just get it. A freaking genie in a bottle.

We'll leave the philosophical questions aside for now. Why is all of this happening? Who needs it? Will robots replace us? Maybe next time.
