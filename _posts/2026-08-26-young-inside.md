I like looking at people and imagining what they looked like when they were young or what they'll look like when they're old. Trying to visualize how their face and appearance will change. It's always really interesting to look at elderly retirees and imagine, guess, what they looked like in their twenties.

(And there's a similar funny thing about overweight people. I also always imagine that inside them there's a really beautiful, healthy person)

So here's what I think: inside the body of every old man or granny, in their mind, their young self is hidden. That is, they feel pretty much the way I do. (Even though from the outside they look totally different)

And I've been picturing myself as a "young man" for a long time now. Even though I look older. Somewhere in my memories, inside, in my self-awareness, of course I have a lot of experience, a lot of knowledge, a huge amount of baggage. But my self-image is, well, around 25 to 30.

That's why, when I talk to older people, I always try to keep this in mind. Yes, there can be irreversible changes in the brain. But in general, they're just like me, they just look beat up.

Take a look at this dude:

[https://youtu.be/656058JxTM0](https://youtu.be/656058JxTM0)

Just watch for 5 to 10 minutes and you'll see confirmation of all my guesses.

I'd like to still be this spry, easygoing, fun-loving nerd 30 or 40 years from now.

And by the way, the story this guy tells is really cool. I recommend checking it out too. It describes a very famous case of a KGB hacker attack on the institute at Berkeley in the 80s. And how this spry astronomer decided not to cut off the hacker's access, but instead came up with traps for him and tracked him for a year.

Anyway, I recommend it. Check it out for yourself.

How old do you feel?
